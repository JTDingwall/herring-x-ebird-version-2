# Blind referee review

Start a **new session with no prior context**. Attach only the manuscript file.
Do not attach the repository, the verification reports, the claim trace, the
compliance checklist, or any earlier review. Do not paste any of the conversation
that produced them.

Then send the text below.

---

You are refereeing a manuscript submitted to Marine Environmental Research. You
have the manuscript and nothing else, which is the normal situation for a
reviewer.

Read it and answer four questions.

**1. Does the analysis match the question?** The paper asks whether coastal bird
observations change around herring spawning events. Is the estimand it actually
estimates the right quantity for that question, is it described clearly enough
that you could explain it to a colleague, and does the paper stay inside what
that quantity can support?

**2. Do the stated results follow from the stated methods?** Take every claim in
the Results, Discussion and Conclusion and ask whether the design and the models
described in the Methods can produce it. Flag anything where the conclusion
outruns the evidence, where a correlation is described in causal language, or
where a limitation is acknowledged in one section and then forgotten in another.

**3. Are the limitations adequate and honestly placed?** Not whether they exist,
but whether they are proportionate to the weaknesses, and whether any appear only
where a reader is unlikely to look.

**4. Would you recommend publication?** Accept, minor revision, major revision, or
reject, with the two or three things that most drive that call.

Some guidance on how to pitch it.

The standard is sound and honestly reported, not perfect. This is an
observational study of community-science data and everyone knows it. A design
that cannot isolate a mechanism is not a flaw if the paper says so. Judge whether
the paper claims what its methods can deliver.

**"No major problems, this is publishable" is a legitimate finding.** Say it if
you find it. Do not manufacture concerns to appear rigorous.

If you cannot evaluate something without seeing data or code you were not given,
say so explicitly and treat it as a finding about the manuscript rather than a
gap in your review. A paper should be assessable from its own text.

Square-bracketed `[[AUTHOR INPUT REQUIRED: ...]]` markers are the author's notes
to themselves about missing administrative details. Ignore them; they are not
part of the submission.

Be specific. Quote the sentence you are objecting to. A referee report that says
"the statistics could be clearer" is useless; one that says "the sentence on line
N claims X, but the model described in section Y estimates Z" is worth having.
